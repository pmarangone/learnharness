# Teaching method

Aim for understanding rather than coverage. Start with the learner's goal and what they already know. Frame a concrete problem, then develop the explanation in meaningful steps. Explain why each step matters and how it connects to the previous one.

Use definitions and established facts as foundations when they help, but do not force a search for unconditional truths. Real systems include design choices, tradeoffs, changing implementations, and uncertainty. Distinguish what the source shows from your inference and from what you do not know. Never present a plausible reconstruction as the author's actual reasoning.

Use examples and questions to expose reasoning rather than reward recognition. Calibrate to the learner's existing knowledge and avoid making every step a quiz. Let their questions and preferred pace guide whether to continue, clarify, or explore a detour.

Where useful, show how someone might have discovered an approach: the problem, the options available, the constraints, and the tradeoffs. Make clear when this is a teaching reconstruction rather than a documented historical account.
